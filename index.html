Show HN: An API for word timestamps from speech, 75% within 20ms

2026-11-17 @ 15:16:45Points: 12Comments: 1

https://quebec.yarn.so), an API that gives you accurate word timestamps from speech. We needed it because cutting a word even 50ms off from its true timing can ruin talking head footage (examples in the write up).

Accurate word timestamps usually come from a forced aligner, which takes the speech audio and a transcript and works out exactly when each word starts and ends. We weren't satisfied with the accuracy of Montreal Forced Aligner (MFA) or new models like ElevenLabs Cue (https://elevenlabs.io/blog/cue-aligner). Giving LLMs access to a waveform/spectrogram didn't help much either.

So we built our own. There are two particularly interesting parts: - We wrote our own decoder around MFA's acoustic model that only lets each word land within 200ms of the rough timings. That made search small enough to solve exactly, so we don't need to use MFA's beam search. - We added a small CNN, built on the first two layers of Whisper Tiny, that nudges each word boundary by up to 50ms. We couldn't find enough hand-labelled speech that allowed for commercial use, so we had Gemini label our own (17k word boundaries). It took a couple of hours and cost under $200. A net trained on Gemini's labels scored the same as one trained on human labels (73.9% vs. 74%).

One way to measure accuracy is % of word boundaries within 20ms of hand labels. Here are the results on Buckeye (hand-labelled American speech that we didn't train on):

- Quebec 75%

- MFA 61%

- ElevenLabs Cue 30%

If you want to try it, it's free until tomorrow night. You can point your agent at our docs at https://quebec.yarn.so, and send audio on its own or with your own transcript. After that, it costs $0.32 per hour with no signup (minimum $10 top-up). We serve it at cost. Caveats: only tested in English and slower than ElevenLabs.

Let us know if Quebec works well for you, and send us any samples that work badly (you can email support at yarn dot so). Also let us know if you've built or found a better aligner. In theory we would just switch to a better aligner, although we would probably just push a bit harder to make ours better.

Thanks!

Show HN: Open-source, real-time object tracking for live video feeds

2026-11-16 @ 21:14:39Points: 83Comments: 4

tamserve runs Meta's SAM 3 on live camera feeds. SAM 3's own tracker handles one recorded video at a time, runs at about 8 fps, and runs out of memory on long videos. tamserve uses SAM 3 to find objects every few frames and a lightweight tracker (EdgeTAM) to follow them in between, with one copy of each model shared across all your cameras.

*On one RTX 4090:*

* *Any object you name:* 4x faster than SAM 3's tracker, at 92% of its accuracy * *People:* 16 cameras at once, 14x less GPU per frame, and slightly more accurate than SAM 3 (89.6 vs 87.8 HOTA) * *Memory:* flat at 3.5 GB on a 3,000 frame video, where SAM 3 runs out at frame 423

*Worth a look if you're building:*

* Warehouse or factory safety (forklifts, pallets, hard hats) * Security or retail camera analytics * Home or elder care monitoring * Robotics, wildlife or lab monitoring * Annotation tools for long videos

`pip install "tamserve[sam3]"`

Please give me any and all feedback or edit tamserve to meet your needs, and a star helps if you find it useful!
