Show HN: bouncemarket – a live prediction market game for a bouncing ball

2026-11-27 @ 15:01:50Points: 91Comments: 34

I made this game! Some technical details:

The entire drop of the ball is simulated server-side, then streamed to each client as server-sent events with a small buffer. Every drop is deterministic from its starting position, so rounds can be replayed exactly for later analysis, but tiny variations in starting position can change the ultimate outcome.

There's some AI traders to keep the market moving, but they drop out in any round that has more than five people. They operate using a naive set of rules that I wrote for them, so they're not too hard to beat -- it's when the market is full of real people that it gets tricky.

Curious to get feedback on anything you would change, and especially whether the mechanics were clear right away or not.

Show HN: I wrote an entire Vim manual over 4 years

2026-11-26 @ 14:44:22Points: 265Comments: 87

When I started using Vim more than a decade ago, I wished there was a manual out there teaching how to use this fantastic editor step by step, beginning with the foundations up to more advanced features. Of course at the time I did the vimtutor, but it was too dense for me to remember much from it. I also read the entire Vim manual (`:help manual`), but I didn't like it: it was too detailed in places (for example `02.8 Finding help` completely lost me for a while), and other features didn't get the treatment they deserved.

So I decided to write my own Vim manual. It took me more than 4 years and 2987 focused pomodoros (1244 hours) of work to finally get it done!

The result:

- 21 chapters showing the most important Vim features step by step.

- 170 exercises with solutions.

- Many real-life examples to show how Vim can be used and configured for your own needs.

- The related Vim's help pages to dig further into each feature.

Most configs are both written in Vimscript and Lua for Neovim users. This book explores the common features between Vim and Neovim, so don't expect anything about Neovim-specific features (like treesitter or LSP for example).

- The landing page: https://themouseless.dev/vim/

- A sample of the book with the entire table of contents and the first three chapters covering most of the basics: https://themouseless.dev/content/learning_to_play_vim_sample....

I wrote this with my bare hands, no LLM involved to spoil the fun.
