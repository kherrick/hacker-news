Show HN: DragDropMath – Learn math by feel

2026-11-27 @ 17:56:28Points: 108Comments: 33

I am a professional physicist and I shuffle symbols in equations and mathematical expressions all day long. During my PhD (almost 20 years ago) I noticed that it is as if the symbols have their own 'physics', which makes the wrong moves impossible and the right ones obvious.

So I created DragDropMath, where you can learn how to simplify mathematical expressions and how to solve equations by dragging symbols around, and only correct moves are allowed, so you get to feel what's right without experiencing (and so learning) wrong moves.

I always wanted to make this, but life got in the way. Then a few weeks ago I saw declarative dragging (https://declarative-dragging.github.io/) on the front page of HN and the spark came back!

It took quite a few rounds to get the feel right, but I think it's close to what happens in my mind when I mentally manipulate these expressions.

A few more points: - There are no ads, no trackers, no accounts (it's a static website and progress is saved in the browser cache). - Teachers can create their own problem sets, encoded in the link. - I plan to add more chapters (trigonometry, complex numbers, some calculus) but I first need to figure out the 'right' mode of interaction. - I just started working on the experience from mobile, so there may still be some rough edges there

I'm curious to know what you think, if you have suggestions for content, if you spot anything that I should fix, if you have feature requests! Thank you in advance

Show HN: bouncemarket – a live prediction market game for a bouncing ball

2026-11-27 @ 15:01:50Points: 331Comments: 115

I made this game! Some technical details:

The entire drop of the ball is simulated server-side, then streamed to each client as server-sent events with a small buffer. Every drop is deterministic from its starting position, so rounds can be replayed exactly for later analysis, but tiny variations in starting position can change the ultimate outcome.

There's some AI traders to keep the market moving, but they drop out in any round that has more than five people. They operate using a naive set of rules that I wrote for them, so they're not too hard to beat -- it's when the market is full of real people that it gets tricky.

Curious to get feedback on anything you would change, and especially whether the mechanics were clear right away or not.
