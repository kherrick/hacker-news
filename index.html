Show HN: Foolscap – a writing app that reads your book and never writes for you

2026-11-19 @ 13:21:20Points: 59Comments: 26

It reads as you write and picks out the structure: characters, ideas, threads, what's left hanging. You can respond to what it picks out, so there's a dialectic between the system and you as the writer. There's also an agent on the side, plugged into the same system, so you can ask about your book and fire off actions through conversation. It never writes into your work.

The system can also follow theorems and proofs and string together their dependencies, but I haven't made that a focus yet. There's no Lean support, for example. If anyone's interested in that, I'd love to hear it.

I want to build this by iterating with the people who use it: a tool that uses AI not to bypass the creative process, but to bring new tools to it. There's a free tier, and if you email hello@foolscap.works I'll send you a code for 50% off. This is my first product, so feedback of any kind is welcome.

Show HN: Mastra Factory, the open-source software factory we use to build Mastra

2026-11-18 @ 16:53:42Points: 118Comments: 37

Almost two years ago, we shared Mastra, our open-source TypeScript agent framework, on Show HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43103073. It got a few hundred upvotes; a couple of weeks later our downloads had doubled, and we were off to the races.

Today we're sharing Mastra Factory, the open-source software factory we built to help maintain Mastra itself.

At Mastra, we now average nearly 4M downloads per week, and with Factory our team now merges hundreds of PRs a week across the mastra-ai GitHub org. Last week, Factory opened and merged 238 PRs, alongside 630 merged PRs from humans.

There's a lot of debate about what a software factory is. For us Factory is a configurable agentic kanban board that's @-taggable in Slack.

Under the hood, it uses Mastra’s durable agents for long-running sessions, our conversational memory system, a markdown based wiki with source citations to incorporate company knowledge, signals that subscribe to PR comments wired up into sessions, and a customizable cloud sandbox with our coding harness (working on supporting Claude Code, Codex etc).

When we started out, we modeled Factory around our existing workflow-oriented process (triage, plan, build, review, shipped). Three months later, we find ourselves mostly driving it through Slack vs a web UI.

We’ve seen more senior engineers kick off sessions in parallel (“triage the 20 oldest issues and let’s evaluate…”). We’re using bug and vuln scanners now, and we’ve closed our repo to outside contributors, asking folks to write detailed issue repros instead.

You can get started with `npm create factory`.

It’s fully open-source, so you can run it entirely on your infra (we also offer a hosted version). Comment here, we would love to know what you think!
